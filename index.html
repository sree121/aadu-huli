    How to play

    A hunt between two unequal sides. A few strong tigers try to eat goats by jumping over them; a big herd of weak goats tries to surround the tigers until none of them can move. Herders in Nepal scratched the board into the dirt and played with pebbles, and in Tamil Nadu, Karnataka and Andhra Pradesh the board is carved into temple corridors and old verandas.

    Setup

    • Bagh-Chal (Nepal): a 5×5 grid of 25 points joined by straight and diagonal lines. The 4 tigers start on the four corners. All 20 goats start off the board.
    • Adu Huli (South India): a triangle crossed by a rectangle, 23 points in all. The 3 tigers start at the apex and on the two inner points just below it. All 15 goats start off the board.
    • Pieces sit on the points where lines meet and move only along the drawn lines.

    Turns

    • Goats move first, then players take turns. Every turn you must move; you can't pass.
    • Phase 1, placement: on each goat turn, place one new goat on any empty point. Goats already on the board can't move until the whole herd is placed. Tigers move normally during this phase.
    • Phase 2, movement: once every goat is placed, the goat player moves one goat along a line to an adjacent empty point.
    • Tigers move one step along a line to an adjacent empty point, or capture.

    Capturing

    • A tiger captures by jumping over one adjacent goat, in a straight line, onto the empty point directly beyond it. The goat is removed and never comes back.
    • You can capture only one goat per turn. There are no chain jumps.
    • Capturing is optional. Tigers can't jump over other tigers, and goats can never jump or capture.

    Winning

    • Tigers win when they have eaten 5 goats.
    • Goats win when it's the tigers' turn and no tiger can move or jump.
    • If the goats have no legal move, the tigers win.
    • No-repeat rule: after all goats are placed, a goat move can't recreate a position that has already appeared. This stops endless shuffling.
    • Draw: 80 moves in a row with no capture after placement ends.

    Strategy for goats

    • Fill the edges and corners first. A goat on the edge can't be jumped from outside the board.
    • Never leave a goat with an empty point directly behind it along a line a tiger can reach.
    • Build solid blocks. Goats that back each other up can't be jumped.
    • Watch for forks, where one tiger threatens two goats at once. You can only save one.

    Strategy for tigers

    • Stay central. The middle points have 8 lines and give the most jumps.
    • Keep the tigers spread out so one wall of goats can't trap them all.
    • Strike during placement, before the herd is organised. Once the goats are all down and packed, captures get rare.
